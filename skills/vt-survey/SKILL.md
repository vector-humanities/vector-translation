# Source survey

Fill in `templates/survey.md` (copy it into the project). Method §2; rules 1 to 4.

## Steps
1. **Edition.** Identify edition and printing; record why this printing. Identify the standard scholarly edition for the concordance.
2. **Rights.** Find the author's death date in an authority file (GND, VIAF, NDL, Library of Congress). Work out status in the country of origin, the UK and EU (life plus 70), and the US (publication date). Record a modern edition's apparatus separately. If the text is not in the public domain where it will be published, stop and tell the translator before anything else.
3. **Images.** Find full-resolution scans (IIIF where offered). Record identifier and URL. Ask the translator before any large download, giving source, file count and size.
4. **Page mapping.** Work out the formula from image number to printed page from two pages; test it on a third.
5. **OCR test.** If OCR exists or can be made, compare one page with the image. Record what it is good for: source, draft to be checked, or search only.
6. **Every page.** Make a contact sheet of all images (for example with `sips` and a short script, or a sub-agent paging through at low resolution). List: front matter including unnumbered pages, each heading at its real page, figures, tables, verse, the author's notes, blank pages. Compare with the printed contents and list every difference.
7. **Existing translations and quotations.** Search the reference manager first, then library catalogues and the web: complete, partial and draft translations; translations into other languages; scholarship that quotes the text with page references. Record coverage and pages. Third-party material is kept privately, never committed to a public repository.

## Report to the translator
Edition and rights in two lines; page count and structure; differences from the printed contents; existing translations; OCR verdict; questions for the conventions sheet; passages that look hard (verse, third languages, notation).
