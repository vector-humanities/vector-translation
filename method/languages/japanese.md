# Notes for Japanese sources

From the Kuki translation (pre-war Iwanami printing, vertical text, old orthography).

## Rights
- Japan: life plus 50 years for authors who died before 1968. The 2018 extension to life plus 70 was not retroactive. Kuki (d. 1941) entered the public domain at the end of 1991.
- Check the rights of a modern edition's editorial apparatus separately from the text.
- Translations into other languages (for Kuki, the French of 1966) are in copyright: consult them, translate from the Japanese.

## Images
- The National Diet Library Digital Collections serve full-resolution spreads through IIIF and return any region: `https://dl.ndl.go.jp/api/iiif/<pid>/R<7-digit image number>/<x>,<y>,<w>,<h>/full/0/default.jpg`. Low resolution is enough for reading; use regions for figures and doubtful characters. Credit: 国立国会図書館 National Diet Library, JAPAN.
- Each image is a two-page spread. Read the right page first, columns right to left. The mapping from image (コマ) to printed page is a formula of the form: odd page p is the left page of image (p+a)/2, even page p the right page of image (p+a+1)/2. Work out *a* from two pages and test it on a third.
- OCR of pre-war vertical text is unusable as a source (rule 1). Keep it for searching.

## Conventions used for Kuki
- Page markers `[p. N]`; image markers `[コマ N]` beside the first page marker on each spread.
- Emphasis dots (圏点) as italics. 甲, 乙, 丙 as A, B, C.
- Insertions into the author's sentences in tortoise-shell brackets 〔 〕, the Japanese editorial convention for insertions by an editor or quoter. The author's own )( sign was considered and rejected because Kuki uses it himself.
- The author's references stayed inline, as printed, so every footnote was the translator's and began "Tr.".
- Verse and classical Chinese: original as printed, the author's reading, and a close English translation in a footnote; the readable body keeps the English.
- Quotations of European authors translated from the author's Japanese, not from the originals, since the Japanese is what the author argued from.
- 我々 as "we"; 思ふ as "I think". Hedges such as であらう kept as hedges.

## Terms
- The English philosophical corpus pulls terms towards its own defaults. 存在 and 有 both drifted to "being" until fixed as existence and being (rule 4). Expect the same with any pair the English tradition collapses.
- Record in the glossary which European term the author is rendering where he says so (偶然 for *Zufall*, *hasard*; 原始偶然 for Schelling's *Urzufall*; 實存 for *Existenz*).

## Book
- Font stack for the Kuki book: EB Garamond, Hiragino Mincho ProN for Japanese, Gentium for Greek, STIX and Apple Symbols for logic and *Book of Changes* hexagram signs. Four rare Chinese characters fell back to STSong. Run the glyph sweep.
